# Pilot Selection: India-Specific Climate-Risk Case Study

## 1. Executive Summary

To transition **Umbrella** from an abstract synthetic proof-of-concept into an authentic, empirically grounded climate-risk case study, an exhaustive evaluation of four candidate district and historical flood event combinations was conducted across India's most flood-vulnerable agrarian belts:
1. **Darbhanga District, Bihar** (July 15 – August 1, 2020 Flood; Peak: July 24–28, 2020) — **SELECTED PRIMARY PILOT**
2. **Samastipur District, Bihar** (July 20 – August 10, 2020 Flood; Peak: July 29, 2020) — **SECONDARY BENCHMARK**
3. **Barpeta District, Assam** (July 10 – July 26, 2020 Brahmaputra Flood; Peak: July 16, 2020) — *Alternative Candidate*
4. **Kendrapara District, Odisha** (August 25 – September 5, 2020 Mahanadi Flood; Peak: August 28, 2020) — *Alternative Candidate*

## 3. Detailed Profile of the Selected Primary Pilot: Darbhanga District, Bihar

### 3.1 Geographic and Demographic Context
- **State**: Bihar (LGD Code: 10)
- **District**: Darbhanga (Census 2011 Code: 215)
- **Headquarters**: Darbhanga Town
- **Coordinates Bounding Box**: $25.7196^\circ \text{N}$ to $26.4464^\circ \text{N}$, $85.6767^\circ \text{E}$ to $86.4161^\circ \text{E}$
- **Geographic Area**: 2,279 $\text{km}^2$
- **Total Administrative Blocks**: 18
- **Selected Pilot Operational Clusters**: 10 representative agrarian blocks spanning upper terraces, middle river corridors, and low-lying saucer depressions (*chaurs*).

### 3.2 Hydrological Mechanism of Flooding
Darbhanga occupies a critical low-lying saucer depression in North Bihar, situated downstream of the Nepal Terai Himalayan foothills. The district's flood dynamics are governed by four intersecting factors:
1. **Transboundary Catchment Cloudbursts**: Heavy monsoon precipitation across the upstream catchments of the Bagmati, Kamla Balan, and Adhwara rivers in Nepal causes rapid cresting within 24 to 48 hours.
2. **Embankment Vulnerability**: Over 80% of river reaches are artificially channelized by flood protection embankments. When water levels exceed design thresholds, hydrostatic pressure and seepage cause breaches (as occurred at Dewasi in Hayaghat and Madanpur in Biraul).
3. **Natural Saucer Depressions (*Chaurs*)**: Blocks such as Kusheshwar Asthan and Biraul lie in natural depressions (elevation 42 m AMSL) where drainage is congested for 30 to 60 days following crest discharge.
4. **Railway and Highway Chokepoints**: The Samastipur–Darbhanga railway corridor crosses major floodplains; submergence of Railway Bridge 16/17 halts regional transport, paralyzing MFI field operations.

### 3.3 The Benchmark Event: July 15 – August 1, 2020 Flood (`IND-BIH-2020-07`)
- **Event Phase Timeline**:
  - **July 15–17, 2020 ($T-7$)**: Pre-flood rising limb; initial localized waterlogging.
  - **July 18–21, 2020 ($T-5$ to $T-3$)**: Massive cloudburst in upper catchments (ERA5 cumulative rainfall >180 mm); soil moisture saturation exceeding 0.43 $\text{m}^3/\text{m}^3$.
  - **July 23, 2020 ($T-1$)**: Bagmati and Kamla Balan breach critical Danger Levels; road links on NH-527C severed near Keoti.
  - **July 24–25, 2020 ($T_0$ / Peak)**: CWC Hayaghat gauge crests at record **50.82 m** (+2.14 m above Danger Level 48.68 m, surpassing historical Highest Flood Level of 50.60 m). East Central Railway halts all train operations due to submerged tracks at Hayaghat Bridge 16. Embankments breached at Dewasi. Over 1.45 million people impacted across 14 blocks.
  - **July 27–29, 2020 ($T+3$)**: Post-peak backwater inundation spreading across saucer depressions (Kusheshwar Asthan, Biraul); standing water in paddy fields causing total crop failure.

### 3.4 Microfinance Institutional (MFI) Importance
Darbhanga is a high-volume microfinance hub with thousands of Joint Liability Groups (JLGs) predominantly composed of women smallholder farmers and agricultural laborers:
- **Typical JLG Loan**: ₹35,000 to ₹50,000 per borrower, repaid weekly or monthly.
- **Agricultural Exposure**: Over 70% of loans fund Kharif paddy transplanting, fertilizer, seed stock, and makhana (foxnut) harvesting.
- **The Microfinance Dilemma**: When flash riverine inundation strikes, field collection agents cannot physically reach villages due to severed bridges and submerged roads. If the MFI relies on crude uniform portfolio alerts, safe borrowers are penalized, while genuinely devastated borrowers face automated penalties instead of proactive repayment flexibility.
- **Umbrella's Role**: By cleanly separating pure physical flood hazard from portfolio balance, Umbrella enables credit committees to grant auditable repayment grace periods without distorting underlying climate hazard science.
